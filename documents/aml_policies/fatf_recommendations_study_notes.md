## 1 About the FATF

The Financial Action Task Force (FATF) is an inter-governmental body that sets international standards to combat money laundering, terrorist financing and proliferation financing. Its standards are the 40 FATF Recommendations, and countries are assessed on them through mutual evaluations. These notes paraphrase key ideas for study purposes only; consult the official FATF text for authoritative wording.

## 2 Risk-based approach (Recommendation 1)

Countries and financial institutions should identify, assess and understand their money laundering and terrorist financing risks and apply measures proportionate to those risks, with enhanced measures where risks are higher.

## 3 Customer due diligence (Recommendation 10)

Financial institutions should not keep anonymous accounts. They should identify and verify customers and beneficial owners, understand the purpose of the relationship, and conduct ongoing due diligence. The FATF standard includes a designated threshold of USD/EUR 15,000 for occasional transactions.

## 4 Record keeping (Recommendation 11)

Records of transactions and CDD information should be kept for at least five years so that they can be provided swiftly to competent authorities.

## 5 Politically exposed persons (Recommendation 12)

For foreign PEPs, institutions should have risk management systems to identify them, obtain senior management approval, establish source of wealth and source of funds, and conduct enhanced ongoing monitoring. Domestic PEPs require these measures when the relationship is higher risk.

## 6 Wire transfers (Recommendation 16)

Wire transfers should carry accurate originator and beneficiary information throughout the payment chain so that transfers can be traced. A de minimis threshold of USD/EUR 1,000 may be applied for reduced information requirements.

## 7 Suspicious transaction reporting (Recommendation 20)

If a financial institution suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds are the proceeds of crime or related to terrorist financing, it should report promptly to the Financial Intelligence Unit.

## 8 Tipping-off and confidentiality (Recommendation 21)

Institutions and their staff should be protected when reporting in good faith and should be prohibited from disclosing that a suspicious transaction report is being filed.

## 9 Beneficial ownership (Recommendations 24 and 25)

Countries should ensure adequate, accurate and up-to-date information on the beneficial ownership of legal persons and legal arrangements is available to competent authorities in a timely manner.
