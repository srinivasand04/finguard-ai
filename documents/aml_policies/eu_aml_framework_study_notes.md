## 1 Overview

The EU framework has evolved through successive AML Directives. In 2024 the EU adopted a package that includes a directly applicable AML Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1624), a sixth AML Directive and a regulation creating a new EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA) based in Frankfurt. Most of the single rulebook obligations apply from July 2027. These notes are a study summary, not legal text.

## 2 Customer due diligence thresholds

Obliged entities apply CDD when establishing a business relationship and for occasional transactions of EUR 15,000 or more, and for occasional transfers of funds above EUR 1,000. The Funds Transfer Regulation requires information on payer and payee to accompany transfers of funds.

## 3 Beneficial ownership

A beneficial owner of a legal entity is generally a natural person who ultimately owns or controls it, with ownership of more than 25% of shares or voting rights as an indication. Member States maintain central beneficial ownership registers.

## 4 Cash limit

The AML Regulation introduces an EU-wide limit of EUR 10,000 on cash payments for goods or services by traders, with Member States able to set lower limits.

## 5 High-risk third countries

The European Commission identifies high-risk third countries with strategic deficiencies in their AML regimes. Obliged entities must apply enhanced due diligence to business relationships and transactions involving those countries.

## 6 Role of AMLA

AMLA coordinates national supervisors and financial intelligence units and will directly supervise a limited number of the highest-risk cross-border financial institutions.
